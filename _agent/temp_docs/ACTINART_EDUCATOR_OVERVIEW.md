# Indiana Art Activist Directory (ACT.IN.ART.)
## Educator-facing overview & classroom use guide

ACT.IN.ART. is a curated, data-rich directory of Indiana-based activist artists, collectives, and artworks designed to support **civic learning, media literacy, local history, and arts education**.

The site’s mission is to:
- **inspire change through art**, and
- **influence how we think about present-day issues**  
  by connecting artworks to real causes, places, and historical moments.

For educators, that mission translates into a local-first teaching resource that is rigorous, flexible, and project-friendly.

---

## Why this resource is valuable in classrooms

### Local relevance
Students often engage more deeply when activism is:
- nearby,
- tied to recognizable places,
- and connected to their lived environment.

ACT.IN.ART. anchors abstract ideas in Indiana communities.

### Cross-disciplinary fit
The directory supports:
- visual arts,
- social studies and civics,
- ELA and argumentation,
- media studies,
- data literacy.

---

## Core features educators can use

### Directory-based inquiry
Students can explore by artist, cause, medium, and location—then build research or creative responses from a stable, curated starting point.

### Curated collections
Collections act like mini digital exhibitions. They are especially helpful for:
- quick lesson launches,
- small-group thematic research,
- comparative analysis across causes.

### Timeline+
The timeline helps students track:
- change over time,
- relationships between local and national movements,
- cause evolution across decades.

### Map + tours
Place-based learning becomes realistic and engaging:
- students can locate works near their town,
- design teacher-approved virtual field trips,
- or build research around the relationship between civic identity and place.

### Analytics
Simple charts support:
- pattern detection,
- critical questions about representation,
- thoughtful discussion of dataset bias and growth needs.

### Search+
Fuzzy, synonym-aware search reduces barriers for:
- unfamiliar vocabulary,
- abbreviations,
- younger learners and multilingual contexts.

## Classroom-ready activity ideas

1. **Local activist art profile**
   - Students select one entry and produce:
     - an artist + artwork summary,
     - a cause explanation,
     - a source-quality evaluation.

2. **Cause-based micro-exhibition**
   - Groups curate 5–8 entries and write:
     - a shared curatorial statement,
     - a short “why this matters in Indiana” reflection.

3. **Map narrative**
   - Students choose 3–5 works in a region and write:
     - a place-based story connecting art to community context.

4. **Data critique workshop**
   - Students use analytics to:
     - identify skews,
     - propose reasons for gaps,
     - recommend additions with citations.

## Zine Builder as a signature tool

The Zine Builder allows teachers and students to generate printable classroom materials:
- from curated collections,
- from student-selected lists,
- or from a class-wide theme.

This supports gallery walks, presentations, and civic showcase nights.

---

## Safe community participation

ACT.IN.ART. includes a contribution system that invites:
- new-entry proposals,
- corrections,
- takedown requests,
- reflections.

Local-first moderation prototypes make it possible to model responsible digital stewardship in the classroom before a future CMS workflow is added.

---

## Accessibility & equity

The design direction prioritizes:
- clean navigation,
- alt-text culture,
- screen-reader-friendly patterns,
- a strong future path to multilingual support.

---

## Rights & respectful use

The directory is intended to support education and public understanding.  
When using images in classroom materials:
- prioritize attribution,
- use excerpts for discussion rather than full reproduction where uncertain,
- honor artist requests and takedown workflows.

---

## The teaching thesis
**Students learn civic imagination faster when they can see it in their own state.**

ACT.IN.ART. offers a structured way to explore that imagination—and to turn local activist art into meaningful, rigorous learning.

# ACT.IN.ART.
## Indiana Art Activist Directory — Educator Handout

**ACT.IN.ART.** (Activism Indiana Artistry) is a curated, data-rich directory of Indiana-based activist artists, collectives, and artworks.  
It supports **civic learning, local history, media literacy, and arts education** through a local-first lens.

This handout is designed for quick adoption in classrooms, clubs, and community learning programs.

---

## Why ACT.IN.ART. works in schools

### Local relevance increases engagement
Students often connect more deeply when activism and culture are visible in familiar places. ACT.IN.ART. helps students see that civic imagination is not abstract—it is present across Indiana cities, campuses, neighborhoods, alleys, and public spaces.

### Naturally cross-curricular
The directory supports:
- **Visual arts:** medium, public art, design choices, audience impact  
- **Social studies & civics:** movements, rights, civic identity, local history  
- **ELA:** argumentation, research synthesis, narrative and reflection  
- **Media literacy:** evaluating sources, framing, and credibility  
- **Data literacy:** interpreting patterns in analytics and discussing bias

## Core features you can teach with

1. **Directory**
   - Research by artist, cause, medium, or location.  
   - Use profiles as stable starting points for inquiry.

2. **Curated collections**
   - Mini digital exhibitions for rapid lesson launches.

3. **Timeline+**
   - Supports “change over time” analysis and local/national comparisons.

4. **Map + tours**
   - Place-based learning and teacher-approved virtual field trips.

5. **Analytics**
   - Chart-reading and critical reasoning about representation.

6. **Search+**
   - Fuzzy, synonym-aware searching reduces student frustration.

7. **Zine Builder**
   - Turns research into printable, student-owned artifacts.

## Quick-start classroom activities

### 1) Local activist art profile (1–2 class periods)
Students select one entry and produce:
- a short artist + artwork summary  
- a cause explanation  
- a source-quality review of linked media

### 2) Cause-based micro-exhibition (3–5 class periods)
Small groups:
- select 5–8 entries  
- write a curatorial statement  
- present a “Why this matters in Indiana” rationale

### 3) Map narrative (2–3 class periods)
Students:
- choose 3–5 works in a region  
- write a place-based story connecting art to community context

### 4) Data critique workshop (1–2 class periods)
Using analytics, students:
- identify skews in the dataset  
- propose reasons for gaps  
- recommend new entries with citations

## Safe community participation

ACT.IN.ART. includes a contribution system for:
- new-entry proposals  
- corrections  
- event suggestions  
- reflections  
- takedown requests

Local-first moderation prototypes allow educators to model responsible digital stewardship before future CMS workflows are adopted.

---

## Rights-aware classroom use

The directory is educational in purpose.  
When using images for assignments:
- prioritize attribution  
- use excerpts when rights are unclear  
- honor takedown requests

---

## The teaching thesis

**Students learn civic imagination faster when they can see it in their own state.**  
ACT.IN.ART. gives educators a structured way to connect art, history, and present-day issues inside a local, credible, research-friendly resource.

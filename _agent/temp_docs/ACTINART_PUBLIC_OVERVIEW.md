# Indiana Art Activist Directory (ACT.IN.ART.)
## A public guide to what this site is and why it exists

The Indiana Art Activist Directory is a living, growing directory of Indiana-based artists, collectives, and artworks that advocate for social, civic, environmental, and human-rights causes.

The goal is simple and ambitious:

**to inspire people in Indiana to create change through art, and to influence how we think about present-day issues by making activist art visible, understandable, and easy to explore.**

ACT.IN.ART. (abbreviated for **Activism Indiana Artistry**) is not just an archive. It is designed as a public “activation engine” that helps you discover, understand, connect, and participate.

---

## What you can do here

### Explore the directory
Browse entries by artist, cause, medium, location, or timeframe. Each profile aims to include:
- an artist or collective overview,
- artwork context and history,
- images and credits,
- links to official websites and media coverage.

### Use the map
The map is for place-based discovery. It lets you see where activism lives across Indiana—murals, performances, installations, exhibitions, and community projects.

### Follow the timeline
The timeline turns isolated entries into a clearer story. It helps you see:
- earlier roots of activist art in the state,
- changing priorities across decades,
- surges in response to major social moments.

### Read the analytics
The analytics pages answer big-picture questions:
- Which causes are most represented right now?
- Which mediums show up most often?
- Which regions have strong public-art footprints?
- Where are the helpful gaps we should fill?

This is not about turning people into charts. It is about making the growth of civic expression visible.

## Mission-forward features

These features build on the directory **without changing your existing homepage layout or identity**:

### Curated collections
Mini digital exhibitions that group entries by theme and provide short introductions to help visitors go deeper than one-off browsing.

### Search+
A friendlier discovery tool that can handle fuzzy matches and familiar shortcuts (like “BLM”) so people do not need exact titles to find what they need.

### Saved lists and sharing
Visitors can save entries and share a link to their list. This supports:
- classroom assignments,
- community groups,
- “here are the pieces that influenced me” recommendations.

### Tours
A lightweight route builder that turns map discovery into intentional exploration. It can be used for walking experiences, regional drives, or virtual tours.

### Zine Builder
A way to generate printable mini-publications from curated lists or collections—ideal for educators, workshops, gallery walks, and community events.

### Events
A calendar that connects the directory to real-time activity across the state: exhibitions, talks, workshops, and community initiatives.

### Reflections
A moderated space where community members can share how specific works affected them—adding the “why it mattered” layer that directories often miss.

---

## What this site is not
- It is not a definitive, final list.  
  It is a **living directory** that grows through research and community input.
- It is not a replacement for artists’ own platforms.  
  It is a discovery and context layer that points to official sources.
- It is not meant to flatten complex movements into simple tags.  
  Categories are navigation tools, not a substitute for story.

---

## How you can help
ACT.IN.ART. welcomes:
- suggestions for new entries,
- corrections and link updates,
- additional media coverage,
- reflections that document real impact,
- takedown requests when needed for rights and safety.

---

## A note on respect and rights
Images and information are presented with an educational intent. When rights or permissions are unclear, the site aims to:
- credit sources responsibly,
- use careful rights labeling,
- respond quickly to correction and takedown requests.

---

## The big idea
Art activism is not something that only happens “elsewhere.”  
It is in neighborhoods, alleys, galleries, campuses, streets, and hometowns across Indiana.

This website exists to make that visible—and to help more people join the story.
